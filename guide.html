[CHAPTER 01 • STATUTORY ENTITY MATRIX]

Chapter 1: The Complete Indian Entity & Registration Matrix

This is the definitive, statutory index of every legal business structure available in India. It includes standard commercial entities, specialized corporate shells, non-profits, and obscure legal structures defined under the Companies Act, 2013 and allied Indian statutes.

1. Unincorporated Entities (High Personal Risk)

These structures do not possess a separate legal identity from their owners. If the business defaults on debt or faces a lawsuit, the founder’s personal assets (house, savings) are liquidated to cover the liability.

Sole Proprietorship

  • Governing Statute: None (governed by local laws like the Shops & Establishments Act).
  • Liability: Unlimited personal liability.
  • Founder Context: Optimal for Day-1 solo consultants and freelancers to minimize compliance overhead. Taxes are filed under the founder’s personal PAN card.

Partnership Firm (Unregistered & Registered)

  • Governing Statute: The Indian Partnership Act, 1932.
  • Liability: Unlimited, joint, and several liability (one partner can be held personally liable for the other partner's actions).
  • Founder Context: Highly dangerous for deep-tech or capital-intensive ventures due to personal liability. Replaced almost entirely by the LLP model.

Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)

  • Governing Statute: Hindu Law.
  • Liability: Unlimited for the Karta (head of the family); limited to the family share for Coparceners (members).
  • Founder Context: Strictly a traditional family wealth-management and tax-shielding vehicle. Not used for raising external venture capital.

2. Limited Liability Partnerships (The Hybrid)

Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)

  • Governing Statute: The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008.
  • Liability: Limited to the agreed contribution; partners are shielded from the independent unauthorized actions of other partners.
  • Founder Context: Excellent for B2B service firms, asset-holding companies, and investment SPVs. However, it cannot issue ESOPs and faces severe friction when raising venture capital, as institutional VCs prefer private limited companies.

3. Companies Limited by Shares (The Venture Standard)

These are corporate entities governed by The Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(22)]. The liability of shareholders is strictly limited to the unpaid amount on the shares they hold.

One Person Company (OPC)

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(62)].
  • Structure: Only one single founder/shareholder is permitted. Must nominate a successor.
  • Founder Context: A corporate upgrade to the Sole Proprietorship offering limited liability. However, it cannot issue equity to co-founders or investors without converting to a standard Pvt Ltd.

Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd)

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(68)].
  • Structure: Minimum 2, maximum 200 shareholders. Strictly prohibits public trading of shares.
  • Founder Context: The absolute gold standard for scalable startups. It allows for complex capitalization tables, ESOP pools, Preference Shares (CCPS) for VCs, and 100% automatic-route Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).

Public Limited Company (Ltd)

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(71)].
  • Structure: Minimum 7 shareholders, no maximum limit. Shares are freely transferable.
  • Founder Context: The mandatory structure for raising capital from the public via an Initial Public Offering (IPO) on the BSE/NSE. It faces extreme regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs.

4. Obscure Corporate Structures (Companies Act, 2013)

Company Limited by Guarantee

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(21)].
  • Structure: Members do not buy shares; instead, they guarantee to contribute a fixed monetary amount only if the company is wound up.
  • Founder Context: Rarely used for commercial startups. Typically utilized by exclusive clubs, trade associations, or research bodies that require corporate status without raising equity capital.

Unlimited Company

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(92)].
  • Structure: A registered corporate entity where members have absolutely no limit on their liability if the company goes bankrupt.
  • Founder Context: Extremely rare. Sometimes used for highly specific professional service firms or to bypass certain statutory capital restrictions, but generally avoided due to the catastrophic personal risk.

5. Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) & Non-Profits

Founders attempting to commercialize deep-tech IP through these entities will face immediate regulatory roadblocks, as none of these structures can distribute dividends or issue equity.

Section 8 Company

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 8].
  • Structure: Operates like a Pvt Ltd but strictly prohibits dividend payouts. All profits must be reinvested into its charitable/scientific mandate.
  • Founder Context: The most transparent and trusted non-profit structure. Ideal for pure research institutes, but impossible to use for a commercial exit.

Public Charitable Trust

  • Statute: Indian Trusts Act, 1882 (and state-specific acts).
  • Structure: Property/funds handed over to a Board of Trustees for a charitable purpose via a Trust Deed.

Society

  • Statute: Societies Registration Act, 1860.
  • Structure: A democratic organization of at least 7 members managed by a governing council (often used for housing boards or academic societies).

6. Specialized & Sector-Specific Entities

Producer Company

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 378A].
  • Structure: Designed exclusively for farmers and agriculturists (requires 10+ members). Combines the limited liability of a corporate entity with the democratic voting framework of a cooperative.

Nidhi Company

  • Statute: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 406].
  • Structure: A specialized non-banking financial entity. It is legally restricted to borrowing from and lending to its own members to cultivate saving habits.

Co-operative Society (State & Multi-State)

  • Statute: State Co-operative Acts / Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002.
  • Structure: Formed to protect the economic interests of its members (e.g., Amul). Highly regulated by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies.

7. Foreign Entry Vehicles (FEMA/RBI Regulated)

For foreign companies establishing a footprint in India without incorporating a new Indian subsidiary.

Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS)

  • Technically a standard Pvt Ltd or Public Ltd company where 100% of the shares are held by a foreign parent entity. This is the most robust entry vehicle.

Branch Office (BO)

  • An extension of the foreign parent company allowed to execute identical commercial, manufacturing, or trading activities inside India.

Liaison Office (LO)

  • A strict communication channel. It can explore the market and connect with Indian vendors, but is legally barred from generating any commercial revenue in India.

Project Office (PO)

  • A temporary corporate shell established by a foreign company purely to execute a single, pre-awarded contract in India. It must be dissolved when the project concludes.
[CHAPTER 02 • FINTECH INFRASTRUCTURE & SETTLEMENT RAILS]

Chapter 2: Indian Payment Platforms, Gateways & Infrastructure Directory

Here is the consolidated directory of every payment platform, gateway, and infrastructure provider discussed, categorized by their structural utility.

*Note on Indian Pricing:* Under RBI and NPCI mandates, standard UPI transactions carry a Zero MDR (Merchant Discount Rate). For traditional gateways, domestic Credit/Debit/Netbanking standardizes around 1.8% to 2.0%, while international cards sit at 3.0% to 4.3%. All fees listed below are exclusive of India's mandatory 18% GST on financial services.

1. Creator Monetization & Digital Storefronts

These platforms provide the UI (landing pages, course hosting, file delivery) and layer their platform fee on top of standard payment gateway charges.

Platform Core Advantage (The Differentiator) Standard Pricing/Charges
Cosmofeed All-in-one link-in-bio, events, and Telegram/Discord community monetization. 10% flat platform fee + 18% GST + standard PG fees.
Getvik High-volume storefront alternative to Cosmofeed with tiered subscription pricing to cap percentage bleeds. Free tier: 10%. Paid tiers: ₹299/mo for 5% fee, or ₹1,499/mo for 3% fee.
TagMango Built-in DRM for anti-piracy, cohort-based course hosting, and WhatsApp marketing funnels. Custom percentage (typically 10%) or tiered SaaS pricing based on creator volume.
Graphy White-labeled academy builder where the creator owns 100% of the audience data and domain. 10% flat fee, or SaaS tiers (starting ~$20/mo) with lower transaction fees.
Tips On Stream OBS-integrated micro-transactions allowing streamers to bypass 30% YouTube Super Chat cuts. 10% flat fee with zero-threshold Sunday bank settlements.
Instamojo Instant payment links and basic file-hosting for MSMEs without needing a custom website. 5% + ₹3 per transaction for digital goods (or standard 2% for core PG).

The core infrastructure for standard e-commerce, SaaS, and B2B websites.

Airpay Supports 140+ payment instruments across web, mobile, and IVR channels. ~2% Domestic; Custom enterprise pricing.

Built to bypass the high failure rates and opaque FX markups of domestic gateways when accepting foreign payments or repatriating export revenue.

Stripe India The global developer standard; seamless multi-currency support (135+ currencies). 2% Domestic; 3% International + 2% currency conversion fee.

Winvesta Provides localized foreign bank accounts (USD, GBP, EUR) to collect like a local entity. ~$3 per incoming wire + ~1.5% FX conversion.
Skydo Zero FX markup model for IT service exporters and freelancers. Flat fee per invoice (e.g., $19 to $29) regardless of transaction size.
PayGlocal Indian-built gateway specifically optimizing global credit card acceptance rates. 3.5% to 4.5% (Custom volume pricing).
PingPong High-volume repatriation for Amazon global sellers and IT exporters. Capped at 1% or less (Highly aggressive for e-commerce).
Adyen / Checkout.com True global acquiring networks for massive enterprises (Spotify, Microsoft). Interchange++ pricing model (Enterprise custom).
Payoneer / Wise Freelancer and agency standards for cross-border payouts. Wise: Mid-market rate + ~0.6%. Payoneer: ~1% to 2% FX fee.
