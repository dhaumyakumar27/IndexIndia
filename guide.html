01. Unincorporated & Direct-Tax Specific Entities

These structures do not possess a separate corporate identity from their creators or are recognized primarily as distinct taxable units ("Persons") under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (erstwhile Section 2(31) of the Income-tax Act, 1961).

Sole Proprietorship

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: No dedicated corporate statute; recognized via localized commercial registrations (MSME Udyam Registration, state-specific Shops & Establishments Acts, or GST).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 1 resident Indian individual with a personal PAN card and bank account. Zero minimum capital requirement.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ₹0 for online MSME Udyam registration (~15 minutes); ~₹1,000–₹5,000 and ~2–7 days if adding a local Shop & Establishment license or GST registration.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Unlimited personal liability (personal house and savings are exposed to business creditors). Taxed at individual slab rates; eligible for presumptive taxation (50% deemed profit on gross receipts up to ₹75 Lakhs/year for professionals under erstwhile Section 44ADA / Section 58 of the 2025 Act). Mandatory GST registration triggers only at ₹20 Lakhs turnover for services or ₹40 Lakhs for goods.
  • CONTEXT: Optimal for Day-1 solo researchers, consultants, and freelancers keeping overhead near zero before crossing commercial liability triggers.
  • EXAMPLE: An independent bioinformatics researcher developing a proprietary algorithmic model for protein-folding prediction.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Obtain Udyam (MSME) Registration: Log in to the official portal (udyamregistration.gov.in), authenticate your personal identity and PAN, select your NIC (National Industrial Classification) activity code, enter bank and establishment details, and generate the free e-certificate (~15 minutes).
  2. 2. Secure Local Municipal License (Shop & Establishment): Apply on your state's labor department portal (e.g., ENagar in Gujarat or Aaple Sarkar in Maharashtra) with your rent agreement/NOC and utility bill within 30 days of starting operations.
  3. 3. Register for GST (If Applicable): File Form GST REG-01 on gst.gov.in if your annual turnover crosses ₹20 Lakhs for services or ₹40 Lakhs for goods, or if engaging in inter-state B2B sales.
  4. 4. Open a Current Bank Account: Submit at least two proof-of-business documents (such as the Udyam Certificate + Shop Act license or GST certificate) alongside your personal PAN to open a dedicated current account.

Partnership Firm (Unregistered & Registered)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: The Indian Partnership Act, 1932.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 2 partners, maximum 50 partners (capped via Section 464 of the Companies Act, 2013). Requires a written Partnership Deed on stamp paper. Zero statutory minimum capital.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹2,000–₹5,000 (1–3 days) for an unregistered notarized deed; ~₹5,000–₹15,000 (7–15 days) to register with the State Registrar of Firms (ROF).
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Unlimited, joint, and several personal liability for all partners. Taxed at a flat 30% rate (plus surcharge/cess) with zero corporate shield. Unregistered firms face a severe statutory disability under Section 69: they cannot file lawsuits against third parties to enforce contracts.
  • CONTEXT: Dangerous for deep-tech, R&D, or capital-intensive ventures due to personal liability exposure; largely replaced by the LLP.
  • EXAMPLE: A boutique industrial design studio researching and prototyping ergonomic CAD blueprints for 3D-printed orthopedic casts.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Draft & Execute the Partnership Deed: Draft the deed defining partner names, capital contributions, profit/loss sharing ratios, and dispute resolution clauses. Print it on non-judicial e-stamp paper of the value prescribed by your state's Stamp Act (~₹500 to ₹5,000+), have all partners and two witnesses sign, and notarize it. (An unregistered firm stops after obtaining its PAN and bank account, though it remains barred from suing third parties on contracts under Section 69).
  2. 2. Apply for a Firm PAN & TAN: Submit Form 49A (PAN) and Form 49B (TAN) via Protean (NSDL) or UTIITSL portals using the notarized Partnership Deed as proof of existence.
  3. 3. Register with the State Registrar of Firms (ROF): File Form I on your state's ROF portal along with a certified true copy of the Partnership Deed, partner ID/address proofs, and registered office proof (NOC + utility bill).
  4. 4. Receive Certificate of Registration: Upon verification by the Registrar, the firm's name is entered into the Register of Firms and a formal registration certificate is issued (~7–15 days).
  5. 5. Open Current Account: Present the Firm PAN, Partnership Deed, and ROF Certificate to your bank.

Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Hindu Law / Hindu Succession Act, 1956, and recognized as a separate taxable entity under the Income-tax Act.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 2 coparceners/members belonging to a Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, or Sikh family, headed by a Karta (senior-most manager), plus an HUF Deed and ancestral/gifted corpus.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹2,000–₹5,000 (for HUF Deed, rubber stamp, and HUF PAN application); ~7–15 days to obtain PAN and open an HUF bank account.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Unlimited personal liability for the Karta; limited to their share of family property for Coparceners. Holds a separate PAN card and enjoys its own basic income tax exemption slab.
  • CONTEXT: A traditional family wealth-management, real-estate holding, and tax-splitting vehicle; unsuitable for raising external venture capital.
  • EXAMPLE: A multi-generational family funding a long-term, closed-loop agricultural research project on their ancestral land to crossbreed a drought-resistant heirloom wheat strain.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Execute an HUF Deed: Draft a declaration deed on stamp paper naming the Karta (manager) and all Coparceners (family members), witnessing the creation of the HUF corpus via ancestral property, a will, or a specific gift from a non-member.
  2. 2. Engrave the HUF Rubber Stamp: Create a rectangular stamp reading "For [Name of Karta] HUF — Karta".
  3. 3. Apply for an HUF PAN Card: File Form 49A online under the "Hindu Undivided Family" category, attaching the notarized HUF Deed and the Karta's identity/address proofs.
  4. 4. Open an HUF Bank Account & Demat: Use the HUF PAN, HUF Deed, and HUF stamp to open a dedicated bank account and deposit the initial gift/ancestral seed capital.

Association of Persons (AOP) & Body of Individuals (BOI)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Income-tax Act, 2025 (erstwhile Section 2(31)(v) of the Income-tax Act, 1961).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 2 or more individuals (BOI) or entities/individuals (AOP) executing a joint venture or common income-producing agreement without incorporating a separate company or partnership.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹5,000–₹20,000 (contract/consortium agreement drafting + PAN application); ~5–10 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Joint and several liability among members. If member shares are indeterminate, taxed at the Maximum Marginal Rate (MMR).
  • CONTEXT: Frequently used by EPC/infrastructure engineering consortiums bidding together on government tenders; heavily scrutinized by tax authorities.
  • EXAMPLE: A temporary consortium of independent civil engineers, material scientists, and a local construction firm pooling resources to research and develop a localized, flood-resistant porous asphalt formula to bid on a specific state highway tender.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Execute the Consortium / Joint Venture Agreement: Draft and sign a binding agreement on stamp paper between the participating individuals (BOI) or corporate/individual entities (AOP), clearly specifying whether member profit-sharing ratios are determinate (fixed) or indeterminate.
  2. 2. Apply for an AOP / BOI PAN & TAN: File Form 49A selecting the AOP or BOI status category, attaching the executed agreement and authorized signatory resolution.
  3. 3. Open Escrow / Current Account & Obtain Project GST: Open a dedicated joint-signatory bank account and apply for project-specific GST registration (GST REG-01) to raise invoices for the tender or venture.

Self-Help Group (SHG) & Joint Liability Group (JLG)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: NABARD / RBI Microfinance Guidelines and NRLM / SRLM frameworks.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 10–20 members (for SHGs) or 4–10 members (for JLGs) from similar socio-economic backgrounds pooling micro-savings.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ₹0–₹500; ~1–4 weeks (formation of group resolution and savings bank linkage).
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Mutual/peer-guaranteed joint liability for bank loans. Minimal compliance (internal ledger book).
  • CONTEXT: Grassroots rural micro-enterprise and artisanal manufacturing collectives; often upgraded into a Producer Company or Co-operative at scale.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Convene Founding Meeting & Pass Resolution: Gather 10–20 members (SHG) or 4–10 members (JLG), pass a written resolution in a bound minute book electing two to three group leaders (President, Secretary, Treasurer), and agree on regular savings rules.
  2. 2. Maintain Internal Books (Panchasutra): Record weekly/monthly attendance, savings, internal micro-loans, and repayments for ~3–6 months (or register directly with the local State Rural/Urban Livelihood Mission - SRLM/NULM).
  3. 3. Execute Bank Linkage: Submit the group resolution, member KYC proofs, and bylaws to a local commercial or cooperative bank branch to open a group savings/credit account under NABARD's SHG-Bank Linkage guideline.

Local Authority & Artificial Juridical Person (AJP)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Income-tax Act, 2025 (erstwhile Section 2(31)(vi) & (vii) of the 1961 Act) and respective municipal/university statutes.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Created by state notification, royal/legislative charter, or religious consecration (e.g., Panchayats, Municipalities, Cantonment Boards, Port Trusts, statutory Universities, Bar Councils, and consecrated Temple Deities/Idols).
  • CONTEXT: Residual statutory classifications ensuring every non-human legal entity capable of owning property or suing/being sued is captured within the Indian tax net.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Establish Statutory / Consecrated Charter: Created via state government gazette notification (for Municipalities/Panchayats), legislative charter (for Universities/Bar Councils), or formal religious consecration/endowment deed (for Temple Deities).
  2. 2. Obtain Entity PAN & Tax Exemption: File Form 49A under the "Local Authority" or "Artificial Juridical Person" category using the Gazette Notification, Charter Act, or Endowment Deed, followed by relevant Income-tax exemption filings (such as Section 10(20), 10(23C), or 12AB).

02. The Trust, Endowment & Capital Pooling Architecture

Trusts are fiduciary legal arrangements where a Settlor transfers property/capital to a Trustee to manage for Beneficiaries under a registered Trust Deed.

Public Charitable / Religious Trust (NGO)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: State-specific Public Trust Acts (e.g., Maharashtra/Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950; Gujarat Public Trusts Act) or general principles of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 1 Settlor (Author) and a minimum of 2 to 3 Trustees (no upper limit). Requires a token initial settlement corpus (typically ₹1,000–₹10,000) and a registered Trust Deed.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹5,000–₹20,000; ~7–21 days for sub-registrar deed registration (plus ~1–3 months if registering with a State Charity Commissioner and obtaining Income Tax 12AB / 80G and CSR-1 exemptions).
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Trustees carry fiduciary liability for breach of trust. Income is tax-exempt if at least 85% of annual receipts are applied to charitable objects in India and 12AB registration is maintained. Annual audit (Form 10B/10BB) costs ~₹10,000–₹30,000/year.
  • CONTEXT: Ideal for philanthropic foundations, hospitals, and schools, but irrevocable and legally unusable for commercial equity exits or dividend payouts.
  • EXAMPLE: A purely philanthropic research institute developing and open-sourcing low-cost, decentralized water purification filtration systems using locally sourced clay and moringa seeds for rural villages.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Draft the Trust Deed: Prepare the Trust Deed on non-judicial stamp paper specifying the Settlor, at least 2–3 Trustees, the initial settled property/cash corpus, charitable objects, and an irrevocability clause.
  2. 2. Register at the Sub-Registrar Office: Schedule an appointment at the local Sub-Registrar of Assurances. The Settlor, Trustees, and two witnesses appear in person with ID proofs and photographs to execute and register the deed (~7–21 days).
  3. 3. Register with the State Charity Commissioner (Where Applicable): In states with dedicated Public Trust Acts (such as Maharashtra or Gujarat), file an application with the Charity Commissioner's office to obtain a Public Trust Registration Certificate.
  4. 4. Obtain Trust PAN & Bank Account: Apply for a PAN in Form 49A (under the "Trust" category) and open a trust bank account.
  5. 5. Secure Tax & CSR Clearances: Register on NGO Darpan (NITI Aayog), file Form 10A / 10AB on the Income Tax portal for Section 12AB (tax exemption) and Section 80G (donor tax deduction), and file Form CSR-1 on the MCA portal to accept corporate CSR grants.

Waqf (Islamic Charitable / Religious Endowment)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: The Waqf Act, 1995 (as amended by the Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 1 Waqif (dedicator) permanently dedicating movable or immovable property for pious, religious, or charitable purposes, managed by a Mutawalli (manager) and registered with the State Waqf Board.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹5,000–₹15,000; ~1–3 months for Waqf Board verification and registration.
  • CONTEXT: Permanent, inalienable religious/charitable asset dedication; strictly non-commercial.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Execute the Waqfnama (Endowment Deed): The Waqif (founder) executes a formal Waqfnama on stamp paper permanently dedicating movable or immovable property for religious, pious, or charitable purposes, appointing a Mutawalli (manager) and defining the succession rules.
  2. 2. File Registration Application with the State Waqf Board: Within 3 months of creation, the Mutawalli submits an application under Section 36 to the State Waqf Board (and central UMEED portal) attaching the Waqfnama, property title deeds, revenue records, and encumbrance certificates.
  3. 3. Inquiry & Gazette Entry: The Waqf Board conducts a field survey/inquiry, verifies title and public notice, and enters the endowment into the official Register of Auqaf, issuing a Waqf Registration Certificate.

Private Trust (Family Office & Estate Engine)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: The Indian Trusts Act, 1882.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: 1 Settlor, at least 1 Trustee (corporate or individual), and specified Beneficiaries (individuals or corporate entities).
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹10,000–₹50,000+ (depending on professional structuring and state stamp duty on settled assets); ~3–10 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Specific Trusts (where beneficiary shares are fixed and known) are taxed as pass-through vehicles at the beneficiaries' rates; Discretionary Trusts (where trustees decide payouts) are taxed at the Maximum Marginal Rate (~39%–42.74%).
  • CONTEXT: Used by HNI promoters for multi-generational estate planning, ring-fencing personal wealth from business creditors, and holding promoter shares.
  • EXAMPLE: A Category-I Deep-Tech Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) pooling capital from Ultra-High-Net-Worth Individuals (UHNIs) to deploy patient capital into early-stage quantum computing and solid-state battery startups.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Draft the Private Trust Deed: Draft a Specific (Determinate) or Discretionary Trust Deed identifying the Settlor, Trustee(s), defined Beneficiaries, distribution triggers, and protector powers.
  2. 2. Stamp & Register the Deed: Pay applicable state stamp duty. If immovable property is settled into the trust, mandatory registration at the local Sub-Registrar of Assurances is required under Section 5 of the Indian Trusts Act; if only movable assets/cash are settled, notarization or optional registration suffices.
  3. 3. Obtain PAN & Transfer Corpus: Apply for a Trust PAN (Form 49A), open a trust bank/demat account, and transfer the initial corpus or unlisted shares into the Trustee's name held on behalf of the Trust.

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012 + Indian Trusts Act, 1882.
  • STRUCTURE: 95%+ of Indian AIFs are registered as Private Determinate Trusts, with a SEBI-qualified Sponsor and Investment Manager (typically your GP Pvt Ltd / LLP) and an independent Trustee.
  • CORPUS & TICKET SIZE: Minimum ₹20 Crore fund corpus (₹10 Crore for Category-I Angel Funds). Minimum LP investor ticket size is ₹1 Crore (₹25 Lakhs for Angel Funds or employees/directors of the Manager). Max 1,000 investors per scheme (max 200 for Angel Fund deals).
  • SPONSOR SKIN-IN-THE-GAME: Continuing interest of 2.5% of corpus or ₹5 Crore (whichever is lower) for Cat I & II (5% or ₹10 Crore for Cat III; 2.5% or ₹50 Lakhs for Angel Funds).

  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Category I (VC/Deep-Tech) and Category II (PE/Debt) AIFs enjoy statutory tax pass-through status (Section 115UB / 2025 Act equivalent), meaning non-business investment gains are taxed directly in the hands of the LPs. Annual compliance, NISM-certified key investment team requirements, custodian/valuation audits, and trustee fees cost ~₹5 Lakhs–₹20 Lakhs/year.
  • CONTEXT: The mandatory institutional vehicle for pooling external LP capital in India without violating SEBI's ban on unregistered Collective Investment Schemes.

  3. 3. Have a SEBI-registered Merchant Banker review the PPM and issue a due-diligence certificate (for standard Cat I/II funds).

  5. 5. Pay Registration Fee & Receive Certificate: Once SEBI reviews the filing and clears queries (~3–6 months), pay the registration fee (₹5,00,000 for Cat I/II, ₹15,00,000 for Cat III, or ₹2,00,000 for Angel Funds) to receive the Certificate of Registration in Form B, then declare First Close within 12 months.

03. Companies Limited by Shares (The Venture & Commercial Standard)

Governed by The Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(22)], where shareholder liability is strictly limited to any unpaid amount on their subscribed shares.

One Person Company (OPC)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(62)].
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Exactly 1 Shareholder (must be a natural person who is an Indian citizen, resident or NRI—corporate bodies are strictly barred from holding shares under Rule 3), 1 Nominee (who takes over in case of death/incapacity), and at least 1 Resident Director (stayed in India ≥ 120 days). Zero minimum paid-up capital.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹8,000–₹18,000 (via MCA SPICe+ webform); ~7–14 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Full limited liability protection. Taxed at corporate rates (~25.17% under Section 115BAA, plus dividend tax in personal hands upon extraction). Exempt from holding AGMs and requires only 1 board meeting per half-year (if >1 director). Annual compliance cost: ~₹20,000–₹40,000/year.
  • CONTEXT: Built for solo founders signing high-liability commercial contracts without co-founders or external investors. Cannot issue ESOPs or equity to VCs without converting to a Pvt Ltd.
  • EXAMPLE: A solo hardware inventor researching and stress-testing a proprietary, fire-retardant lithium-ion battery casing for electric scooters.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Single Member + Mandatory Nominee: Enter details for 1 natural-person Indian citizen Shareholder/Director and 1 natural-person Nominee (who takes over shares in the event of the sole member's death or incapacity).
  2. 2. Nominee Consent in SPICe+ & e-MoA: Obtain the Nominee's written consent (formerly Form INC-3, now integrated directly into the SPICe+ Part B and e-MoA INC-33 subscriber tables) and attach the Nominee's PAN and address proof alongside a Class-3 DSC.
  3. 3. Suffix Registration: The reserved name in SPICe+ Part A carries the mandatory statutory suffix "(OPC) Private Limited".

Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(68)].
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 2 Shareholders (maximum 200, excluding employees/ESOP holders), minimum 2 Directors (maximum 15; at least 1 must be resident in India for ≥ 120 days). Zero statutory minimum paid-up capital.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹8,000–₹25,000 (zero MCA incorporation fee up to ₹15 Lakhs authorized capital, plus state stamp duty, PAN/TAN, Class-3 DSCs, and professional fees); ~7–14 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Strict limited liability shield. Effective corporate tax is ~25.17% (22% base + surcharge + cess under Section 115BAA). Requires 4 board meetings/year, 1 AGM, statutory audit, DIR-3 KYC, AOC-4, and MGT-7 filings costing ~₹30,000–₹60,000/year.
  • CONTEXT: The undisputed gold standard for venture-backed startups and HoldCos. Supports CCPS, DVR shares, ESOP pools, 100% automatic-route FDI, and DPIIT Startup tax holidays.
  • EXAMPLE: A deep-tech aerospace startup researching, manufacturing, and commercializing a reusable, solid-fuel micro-satellite launch vehicle.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Obtain Class-3 Digital Signature Certificates (DSCs): Secure Class-3 DSCs with video/e-KYC verification for all proposed directors (min. 2, including at least 1 Indian Resident Director) and subscribers (~1–3 days).
  2. 2. Register Business User Profiles on MCA V3: Create "Business User" accounts on mca.gov.in for each director/signatory and associate their DSCs using the MCA E-Signer utility.
  3. 3. Reserve Company Name (SPICe+ Part A): Propose up to 2 distinct names ending in "Private Limited" along with the NIC main business activity code. Name reservation is either submitted separately for ₹1,000 (valid for 20 days after Central Registration Centre approval) or filed jointly with Part B.
  4. 4. Complete SPICe+ Part B & Linked Webforms: Fill SPICe+ Part B (INC-32) for capital structure, DIN allotment, PAN & TAN; draft electronic e-MoA (INC-33) & e-AoA (INC-34) with Table F custom vetoes; complete AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35) for EPFO, ESIC, Professional Tax, and bank account; and auto-generate INC-9 declarations.
  5. 5. Upload Attachments, Affix DSCs & Pay Fees: Attach DIR-2 (consent to act as director), Registered Office proof (NOC + utility bill ≤2 months old + rent agreement), and identity/address proofs. Affix DSCs of directors and certifying professional (CA/CS/CMA/Advocate), and pay the MCA stamp duty and PAN/TAN fee.
  6. 6. Receive Certificate of Incorporation (CoI): The Central Registration Centre (CRC) issues the CoI (Form INC-11) containing the 21-digit Corporate Identity Number (CIN), along with the company's PAN and TAN (~5–14 working days).
  7. 7. Mandatory Post-Incorporation Steps: Hold the first Board Meeting within 30 days and appoint the first Statutory Auditor. File Form INC-20A (Declaration of Commencement of Business) within 180 days after depositing subscribed share capital into the company current account. Issue stamped Share Certificates (Form SH-1) within 60 days.

04. Limited Liability Partnerships (The Hybrid)

The statutory hybrid combining corporate limited liability with partnership operational and pass-through tax flexibility.

Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 2 Partners (no upper limit; can be individuals or body corporates) and minimum 2 Designated Partners (must be individuals, at least 1 resident in India for ≥ 120 days) holding DPINs. Requires a notarized LLP Agreement filed in Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation. Zero minimum capital.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹6,000–₹15,000 (via MCA FiLLiP portal + stamp duty on LLP Agreement); ~7–14 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Liability is strictly limited to each partner's agreed contribution; partners are not personally liable for another partner's independent misconduct. Taxed at a flat 30% rate (plus surcharge/cess), but post-tax profit distributions to partners are 100% tax-free in the partners' hands (no dividend double-taxation). Statutory audit is mandatory only if annual turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs OR total capital contribution exceeds ₹25 Lakhs. Annual filings (Form 8 & Form 11) cost ~₹10,000–₹25,000/year.
  • CONTEXT: Ideal for consulting firms, GP Investment Manager entities, real-estate SPVs, and B2B cash-flow agencies. Poor fit for deep-tech startups because LLPs cannot issue equity shares, CCPS, or standard ESOPs to VCs and engineers.
  • EXAMPLE: A specialized metallurgical testing and failure-analysis lab providing Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) and material fatigue research as a highly paid B2B service to tier-1 automotive manufacturers.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Obtain Class-3 DSCs & MCA V3 Business User Accounts: Secure Class-3 DSCs for all proposed Designated Partners (minimum 2 individuals, at least 1 resident in India) and register their Business User profiles on mca.gov.in.
  2. 2. Reserve Name & File Incorporation via FiLLiP: Reserve the name first via RUN-LLP (₹200 fee, valid 90 days) or apply directly inside the integrated FiLLiP webform. Enter proposed name (ending in "LLP"), registered office address, NIC activity codes, partner contributions, and request allotment of up to 2 DPINs alongside automatic PAN and TAN.
  3. 3. Upload Consent & Obtain Certificate of Incorporation: Attach Form 9 (consent of Designated Partners) and subscriber sheets, affix DSCs of partners and certifying professional, and pay the MCA fee. The CRC issues the Certificate of Incorporation (Form 16) containing the LLPIN (~5–10 working days).
  4. 4. Draft, Stamp & File the LLP Agreement (Form 3 — Mandatory within 30 Days): Draft the LLP Agreement defining partner contributions, profit-sharing ratios, governance, and vesting. Print it on non-judicial stamp paper of the state-mandated value, notarize it, and file it in Form 3 on MCA V3 within 30 days of incorporation (missing this triggers a ₹100/day statutory late fee).

Section 8 Company (Corporate NGO / Research Foundation)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 8].
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 2 Shareholders & 2 Directors (if private) or 7 Shareholders & 3 Directors (if public). Requires a central government license (Form INC-12 / SPICe+) proving objects are exclusively charitable, scientific, educational, or environmental.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹15,000–₹35,000; ~15–30 days (plus ~1–3 months for Income Tax 12AB, 80G, and MCA CSR-1 registrations).
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Limited liability. Strictly prohibits paying any dividends to members. Enjoys 100% tax exemption on surplus reinvested into its mandate (with 12AB) and highest credibility with Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) donors and government incubators. Annual compliance: ~₹30,000–₹60,000/year.
  • CONTEXT: The premier vehicle for academic research foundations, open-source consortiums, and Technology Business Incubators (TBIs), but a dead-end for commercial equity exits.
  • EXAMPLE: A university-backed applied research foundation operating a high-end nanotechnology cleanroom, dedicated to researching carbon nanotube water desalination membranes.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Reserve Name Without "Pvt Ltd / Ltd" Suffix: In SPICe+ Part A on MCA V3, select "Section 8 Company" and propose a name ending in terms such as Foundation, Institute, Association, Forum, Council, or Federation.
  2. 2. Prepare Custom e-MoA (INC-33) & Financial Projections: Inside SPICe+ Part B, draft the e-MoA (Form INC-13 / INC-33) containing mandatory Section 8 non-profit clauses (prohibiting dividends). Attach a 3-year statement of projected income and expenditure, a brief of proposed work, and declarations in Form INC-14 (CA/CS/CMA) and Form INC-15 (promoters).
  3. 3. Obtain License & Certificate of Incorporation: The CRC processes the combined application and issues the Section 8 License (Form INC-16) alongside the Certificate of Incorporation (INC-11), PAN, and TAN (~15–30 days).
  4. 4. Post-Incorporation Tax & CSR Filings: Apply for Section 12AB and Section 80G on the Income Tax portal (Form 10A/10AB), register on NGO Darpan, and file Form CSR-1 on the MCA portal.

Dormant Company (The IP / Asset Vault)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Companies Act, 2013 [Section 455].

Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) & Core Investment Company (CIC)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Act, 1934 [Section 45-IA] + Companies Act, 2013.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Must be a registered Company. Triggered statutorily under the RBI "50-50 Test" when >50% of a company's total assets are financial assets (shares/loans) AND >50% of its gross income comes from those financial assets. Requires a minimum Net Owned Fund (NOF) of ₹10 Crore for standard Base-Layer lending/investment NBFCs (₹2 Crore for pure P2P/Account Aggregator niches). Unregistered CICs (holding group shares with assets <₹100 Crore and not raising public funds) are exempt from RBI registration; Systemically Important CICs (CIC-ND-SI, assets ≥ ₹100 Crore with public funds) require RBI registration.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹3 Lakhs–₹15 Lakhs+ in legal/advisory costs; ~4–9 months for RBI Certificate of Registration (CoR).
  • CONTEXT: Critical watch-out for Holding Companies (Parent X)—if your HoldCo holds startup shares and earns mostly dividends/capital gains without >50% active operating/studio revenue, it risks being classified as an unregistered NBFC.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Incorporate Company & Infuse Net Owned Fund (NOF): Incorporate a Private or Public Limited Company via SPICe+ (INC-32) with financial/investment objects in the MoA, and deposit the mandatory minimum Net Owned Fund (₹10 Crore for standard Base-Layer Investment & Credit NBFCs; ₹100 Crore+ asset threshold with public funds for a registered CIC-ND-SI) into a Fixed Deposit free from any liens in a scheduled commercial bank.
  2. 2. File Online on the RBI Pravaah Portal: Log in to the RBI Pravaah Portal (pravaah.rbi.org.in), complete the NBFC/CIC registration application, and upload the Statutory Auditor's NOF Certificate, CIBIL credit reports of all directors/shareholders, Banker's Confidential Report, Fit & Proper declarations, and a 5-year business plan.

06. Corporate Group & Statutory Relational Classifications

These are statutory statuses applied on top of standard companies or LLPs that drastically alter tax, audit, and investment privileges:

Holding Company [Sec 2(46)] & Subsidiary Company [Sec 2(87)]

  • STATUTORY RULE: Company X becomes a Holding Company of Company A if X controls the composition of A's Board of Directors OR exercises/controls more than 50% of the total voting power of A. Under Section 186(1), Indian companies are barred from making investments through more than 2 layers of investment subsidiaries.
  • COMPLIANCE IMPACT: Both the Holding Company and the Subsidiary automatically lose "Small Company" exemptions under Section 2(85) and must prepare Consolidated Financial Statements (CFS).
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Via Fresh Incorporation (Top-Down): Incorporate the Subsidiary or Joint Venture Company via SPICe+ (INC-32) on MCA V3.   2. 2. Via Share Swap / Acquisition (Bottom-Up Roll-Up): Incorporate Parent X via SPICe+, pass Board and Shareholder resolutions under Section 186 and Section 62(1)(c), execute stamped Share Transfer Deeds (Form SH-4) transferring founders' shares in the existing company to Parent X in exchange for fresh shares allotted by Parent X (filed via Form PAS-3), and update the Register of Members (Form MGT-1).

  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Must be incorporated solely as a Private Limited Company, Registered Partnership Firm, or LLP. Eligible for up to 10 years from incorporation with turnover under ₹100 Crore (expanded up to 20 years and ₹300 Crore turnover for recognized Deep-Tech R&D startups).
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ₹0 government fee via the National Single Window System (NSWS) / Startup India portal; ~2–7 days for DPIIT recognition (~2–6 months if applying to the Inter-Ministerial Board for the Section 80-IAC 3-year 100% income-tax holiday).
  • CONTEXT: Unlocks 80% patent fee rebates, Form 18A fast-track patent examination, GeM tender turnover/EMD exemptions, SISFS/NIDHI grants, and deferred ESOP taxation.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Incorporate Qualifying Entity: First register a Private Limited Company, LLP, or Registered Partnership Firm.
  2. 2. Register on the National Single Window System (NSWS): Log in to nsws.gov.in (linked with startupindia.gov.in), add the "Registration as a Startup" approval to your dashboard, auto-verify CIN/LLPIN and PAN, upload the Certificate of Incorporation, pitch deck/prototype video, patent/IP details (if any), and self-certify compliance.
  3. 3. Receive DPIIT Recognition Certificate: DPIIT reviews the innovation/scalability narrative and issues the Certificate of Recognition with a unique DIPP number (~2–7 working days, ₹0 government fee).
  4. 4. Apply for Section 80-IAC (3-Year Tax Holiday): Once recognized (for a Pvt Ltd or LLP only), submit the Section 80-IAC Application on the NSWS portal to the Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) with audited financials and deck to claim the 100% corporate tax deduction for 3 consecutive years out of 10.

08. Societies & Co-operatives

Democratic and membership-governed vehicles for academic associations, scientific research bodies, and mass producer collectives.

Registered Society

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Societies Registration Act, 1860 (alongside state-specific acts like the Karnataka or Maharashtra Societies Registration Acts).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 7 Founding Members (for a state-level society) or 8 Founding Members from at least 8 different Indian states (for an All-India National Society). Managed by an elected Governing Body / Executive Committee.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹5,000–₹20,000; ~15–45 days with the State Registrar of Societies.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Annual list of governing body members and audited accounts must be filed with the Registrar (~₹10,000–₹25,000/year).
  • CONTEXT: Widely used for academic/scientific associations, professional bodies, R&D autonomous institutes, and residential maintenance associations.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Convene Founding General Body & Draft Memorandum/Rules: Gather at least 7 Founding Members (state society) or 8 Members from 8 different states (All-India society). Pass a resolution adopting Society name, Memorandum of Association (objects & elected Governing Body), and Rules & Regulations (Bylaws).
  2. 2. Submit Application to the State Registrar of Societies: File the signed Memorandum, Rules & Regulations, President's affidavit on stamp paper, member ID proofs, and registered office NOC/utility bill on your state's Registrar of Societies portal (or physically at the District Registrar's office) along with statutory registration fee (~₹50 to ₹2,000).
  3. 3. Receive Society Registration Certificate: Upon scrutiny, the Registrar issues the Certificate of Registration under the Act (~15–45 days), after which the Society obtains its PAN (Form 49A) and opens its bank account.

Co-operative Society (State & Multi-State)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: State Co-operative Societies Acts (for single-state operations) or the Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002 (for multi-state operations).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Minimum 10 to 21 adult members from different families (for a State Co-op, depending on state rules) or minimum 50 individual members from at least 2 different states (or 2+ co-operative societies from different states) for a Multi-State Co-op.

09. Foreign Entry & Offshore-Onshore Vehicles (FEMA / RBI / SEBI / IFSCA Regulated)

For foreign corporations and global funds establishing a presence or deploying capital in India:

Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS) / Foreign Subsidiary

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: Companies Act, 2013 + FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019.
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Standard Indian Pvt Ltd (min. 2 Shareholders, 2 Directors including 1 Indian Resident Director) where 100% (or >50%) of equity is held by the foreign parent (typically via 99.99% parent + 0.01% nominee shareholder to satisfy the 2-member rule). Requires apostilled/legalized foreign parent board resolutions and Form FC-GPR reporting to the RBI via the FIRMS portal upon share allotment.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹35,000–₹1,00,000 (including apostille, FEMA FC-GPR filings, and legal fees); ~15–30 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Treated as an Indian domestic company for tax (~25.17%, vs. 35%+ for a foreign branch), ring-fences the foreign parent from Indian liabilities, and requires annual FLA (Foreign Liabilities and Assets) return filing with the RBI (~₹50,000–₹1,20,000/year). (Note: Excludes Small Company exemptions).
  • CONTEXT: The cleanest, most tax-efficient vehicle for foreign parents building R&D labs, engineering centers (GCCs), or commercial operations in India.
PROCEDURE:
  1. 1. Apostille / Legalize Foreign Parent Documents: Pass a Board Resolution of the Foreign Parent Company authorizing incorporation of the Indian WOS and naming authorized representative plus nominee shareholder (e.g., 99.99% Foreign Parent + 0.01% Nominee). Have Certificate of Incorporation, Charter/Bylaws, Board Resolution, and foreign directors' Passports/Address Proofs notarized and Apostilled (under Hague Convention) or consularized by the Indian Embassy in home country.
  2. 2. File SPICe+ (INC-32) on MCA V3: Onboard at least 1 Indian Resident Director (with PAN and Class-3 DSC), reserve company name in SPICe+ Part A, attach Apostilled foreign subscriber documents in SPICe+ Part B, and receive the Certificate of Incorporation, PAN, and TAN (~15–25 days).
  3. 3. Inward FDI Remittance (SWIFT + FIRC/KYC): Open the Indian WOS current bank account with an Authorized Dealer (AD Category-I) Bank. Wire subscribed share capital from Foreign Parent overseas bank account via SWIFT (purpose code P0006 — foreign direct investment towards equity) and obtain Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) and KYC Report from AD Bank.
  4. 4. File Form FC-GPR on the RBI FIRMS Portal & INC-20A: Create an Entity Master and Business User account on the RBI FIRMS Portal (firms.rbi.org.in), allot shares within 60 days of funds, file Form FC-GPR (with CS certificate and Valuation Certificate) within 30 days of share allotment, and file Form INC-20A on MCA V3 within 180 days.

Branch Office (BO), Liaison Office (LO) & Project Office (PO)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: FEMA (Establishment in India of a Branch Office or a Liaison Office or a Project Office) Regulations, 2016 + Companies Act, 2013 [Section 2(42) & Section 380].
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Foreign parent must have a profit-making track record during the immediately preceding 5 financial years in its home country and a Net Worth of at least USD 100,000 for a BO (3 years profit & $50,000 net worth for LO; or secured executed project contract for PO). Requires Form FNC approval via an Authorized Dealer (AD Category-I) Bank and registration as a Foreign Company (Form FC-1) with the MCA within 30 days.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹50,000–₹1,50,000; ~30–60 days.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: No separate legal identity (foreign parent has 100% unlimited liability for Indian operations). BO permitted to conduct export/import, consultancy, R&D, and software development (barred from retail/manufacturing outside SEZs). Taxed as Foreign Company at 35% base rate (~38.22% effective). LO prohibited from commercial revenue (zero income tax). PO taxed at 35% on project profits. Annual compliance: ~₹50,000–₹2,00,000/year.
PROCEDURE:

Submit Form FNC to an AD Category-I Bank: Prepare Foreign Parent's Apostilled Certificate of Incorporation, MoA/AoA, audited financials proving track-record & net-worth (5 yrs profit + $100k net worth for BO; 3 yrs profit + $50k net worth for LO; or executed Indian project contract + funding proof for PO), and Letter of Comfort. Submit Form FNC to designated AD Category-I Bank.

GIFT City / IFSC Entity (IFSC Company, IFSC LLP, FME & IFSC AIF)

  • GOVERNING STATUTE: International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA) Act, 2019 + SEZ Act, 2005 (and IFSCA Fund Management Regulations, 2022).
  • MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS: Physical office lease inside the GIFT City SEZ (Gandhinagar, Gujarat), SEZ Development Commissioner approval, MCA incorporation (with IFSC exemptions), and IFSCA registration. For a Registered FME (Retail / Non-Retail / Venture Capital), requires minimum net worth (USD 75,000 for Authorized FME / Venture Capital up to USD 500,000–USD 1,000,000 for Registered FMEs) and 1–2 designated Principal/Compliance Officers based in GIFT City.
  • SETUP COST & TIMELINE: ~₹1,50,000–₹5,00,000 for an IFSC Tech/Holding entity (~15–30 days); ~₹10 Lakhs–₹25 Lakhs (~45–90 days) for a full IFSCA Fund Management Entity (FME) + IFSC AIF Trust.
  • LIABILITY & TAX / COMPLIANCE: Treated as a non-resident offshore jurisdiction under FEMA while physically inside India—allowing USD/foreign-currency accounting, free capital convertibility, zero GST on services exported/rendered to offshore units, and a 100% corporate income-tax holiday for any 10 consecutive years out of 15 years under Section 80LA. Annual compliance & SEZ lease maintenance: ~₹2 Lakhs–₹10 Lakhs/year.
  • CONTEXT: The premier onshore alternative to flipping a holding company or venture fund to Delaware, Singapore, or Mauritius.

Six Strategic Intelligence Data Points for the Entity Matrix

To elevate the entity registration matrix from an administrative directory into an institutional-grade decision engine for the IndexIndia platform, every entity profile incorporates six strategic intelligence parameters alongside basic cost, timeline, and statutory context.

1. Technology Readiness Level (TRL) Trigger Point

WHAT IT MEASURES: The exact TRL window (e.g., TRL 1–2 for a Sole Proprietorship vs. TRL 3–4 for a Private Limited Company) where a legal structure becomes mathematically and legally justified.
OPERATIONAL RULE: Operate as an Udyam-registered Sole Proprietorship during TRL 1–2 (ideation, simulation, and basic bench testing) to keep statutory audit and ROC compliance costs near zero. Upgrade to a Private Limited Company at TRL 3–4 only when hitting a hard trigger wire: signing a high-liability commercial pilot contract, hiring core engineers on equity/carry, filing institutional IP assignments, or accepting sovereign grants and external venture capital.

2. The Exit & Liquidation Pathway (Strike-Off Mechanics)

WHAT IT MEASURES: The statutory timeline, cost, preconditions, and regulatory friction required to legally dissolve the entity if the R&D project fails or transitions into a new parent company.
OPERATIONAL RULE: A Sole Proprietorship can be closed in roughly 24–48 hours by surrendering its GST/Udyam registrations and closing its bank account. A Private Limited Company or LLP with nil assets and nil liabilities can be wound up through the centralized C-PACE (Centre for Processing Accelerated Corporate Exit) via Form STK-2 (for companies under Section 248(2)) or Form 24 (for LLPs under Rule 37), typically taking 2 to 6 months once overdue filings are cleared. Conversely, Section 8 NGOs and Public Charitable Trusts cannot simply be struck off or liquidated for founder benefit; their residual assets must be transferred to another non-profit with similar objects.

3. Sovereign Grant & B2B Procurement Eligibility

WHAT IT MEASURES: Whether the legal structure qualifies for central/state R&D grant frameworks and public procurement relaxations.
OPERATIONAL RULE: Only a Private Limited Company, Registered Partnership Firm, or LLP can obtain DPIIT Startup Recognition, which unlocks GeM (Government e-Marketplace) earnest money deposit (EMD) waivers, prior turnover/experience exemptions in PSU tenders, and Form 18A fast-track patent examination. Furthermore, flagship deep-tech grants—including iDEX (Defence), BIRAC BIG (Biotech), MeitY TIDE 2.0, Startup India Seed Fund (SISFS), and the ANRF ₹1 Lakh Crore RDI Fund—either strictly mandate or heavily favor an Indian-owned Private Limited Company (and Section 80-IAC tax holidays apply exclusively to Pvt Ltd companies and LLPs).

4. Cap Table & Synthetic Equity Compatibility

WHAT IT MEASURES: The entity's legal capacity to issue Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS) to venture funds, Differential Voting Rights (DVR) shares, statutory ESOPs (under Section 62(1)(b)), Sweat Equity (under Section 54), and contractually enforceable 100-Point Synthetic Carry Pools / Phantom Stock / SARs.
OPERATIONAL RULE: Institutional VCs and SEBI-registered AIFs rarely invest in LLPs, OPCs, or Partnerships because those vehicles lack share capital divided into distinct classes of shares, making it impractical to enforce liquidation preferences, anti-dilution ratchets, or tax-deferred ESOP pools. While a Synthetic Carry Pool or Phantom Stock agreement can be structured contractually inside an LLP or Pvt Ltd as a cash bonus tied to project profit or exit milestones, true equity conversion requires a Private Limited Company.

5. Hidden Tax Shields vs. Statutory Traps

PRIMARY TAX SHIELDS:
  • Sole Proprietorship & Partnership: Section 44ADA presumptive taxation allows solo professionals/researchers with gross receipts up to ₹75 Lakhs/year (where ≥ 95% of receipts are digital) to declare a flat 50% profit margin without maintaining complex books of account or undergoing a tax audit.
  • Private Limited Company: Unlocks the 22% concessional corporate tax rate (effective ~25.17% under Section 115BAA), Section 35 scientific research deductions, Section 80-IAC 3-year 100% tax holidays for DPIIT startups, Section 80M inter-corporate dividend deductions for holding companies, and the Section 115BBF "Indian Patent Box" (a 10% concessional tax rate on gross royalties earned from patents developed and registered in India).
  • LLP: Avoids dividend distribution double-taxation—once the LLP pays its flat ~30% corporate tax (plus surcharge/cess), post-tax profits distributed to partners are exempt from personal income tax in the partners' hands.
FATAL STATUTORY TRAPS:
  • The RBI "50-50 NBFC Trap" (HoldCos): If a Private Limited Holding Company (Parent X) holds shares in subsidiaries and > 50% of its total assets are financial assets while > 50% of its gross income comes from dividends or capital gains (without active operating/R&D billing), the RBI classifies it as an unregistered Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act.
  • The Section 164(2) Disqualification Trap: Failing to file financial statements (AOC-4) or annual returns (MGT-7) in a company for 3 consecutive financial years automatically disqualifies all its directors from serving on the board of any company for 5 years.

6. Cross-Border FEMA & Exit Friction

WHAT IT MEASURES: How seamlessly the entity can accept 100% Automatic-Route Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), issue convertible instruments to global investors, or execute cross-border structures via GIFT City IFSCA vs. overseas flips.
OPERATIONAL RULE: Private Limited and Public Limited companies enjoy the cleanest automatic-route FDI architecture (via Form FC-GPR on the RBI FIRMS portal). By contrast, Sole Proprietorships, HUFs, OPCs, Nidhi Companies, and Trust NGOs (without MHA FCRA clearance) are barred from accepting foreign equity FDI.

Eight Operational Registration & Statutory Lifecycle Layers

These eight operational registration layers govern the practical execution of business registration in India across MCA V3, State Registrars, RBI, SEBI, and C-PACE.

01 / Pre-Registration Name Forensics & Object-Clause Engineering

Before paying filing fees on the MCA V3 or State Registrar portals, an application must clear three statutory filters that cause the majority of early-stage registration rejections:

1. CRC NAME SCRUTINY & THE 2026 MCA REJECTION RULES:

Name reservation for companies (SPICe+ Part A, ₹1,000 if filed separately; ₹0 if submitted jointly with Part B) and LLPs (RUN-LLP, ₹200) is processed centrally by the Central Registration Centre (CRC) under Rule 8 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 and Rule 18 of the LLP Rules, 2009:

  • Phonetic & Reversed-Root Rejections: Under MCA Name Reservation norms, the CRC rejects names that are phonetically identical, pluralized, tense-altered, or reversed-word variations of existing companies or LLPs (e.g., Advik vs. Adhvik, or Zencare vs. Carezen), even if the applicant submits a No-Objection Certificate (NOC) from the existing entity.
  • IP India Trademark Cross-Verification: The CRC automatically cross-checks proposed names against the IP India Trademark Registry (ipindiaonline.gov.in) across all 45 Trademark Classes. If an identical or deceptively similar registered or pending trademark exists in the relevant class of goods/services, the name is rejected unless accompanied by a formal board resolution/NOC from the trademark proprietor.
  • Regulated & Restricted Words (Rule 8B): Words implying sovereign patronage (Bharat, India, National, Central, Republic, Federal, Prime Minister, Statutory, President) require prior Central Government approval. Financial words (Bank, NBFC, Mutual Fund, Venture Capital, Asset Management, Stock Exchange, FinTech, Insurance, Sovereign, Clearing) require either a prior NOC from the sectoral regulator (RBI, SEBI, or IRDAI) or specific minimum authorized capital thresholds.
2. NIC-2008 / ACTIVITY CODE & OBJECT CLAUSE ALIGNMENT:
  • 5-Digit NIC Code Selection: Every MCA V3 incorporation, Udyam registration, and GST filing requires selecting a 5-digit National Industrial Classification (NIC) code.
  • Downstream Registration Lock-In: If the Main Objects clause in your e-MoA (Form INC-33) or LLP Agreement does not explicitly match your NIC code, downstream portals will reject your applications—for example, RBI Pravaah requires financial objects for NBFC registration, SEBI SI Portal requires investment management objects for an AIF GP, and Udyam distinguishes between Manufacturing and Services based strictly on the NIC code selected.

02 / Capital Structuring & State-Wise Stamp Duty Arbitrage

While the Companies Act, 2013 and LLP Act, 2008 prescribe zero statutory minimum paid-up capital for standard entities, how you split Authorized Capital vs. Paid-Up Capital and which state hosts your Registered Office directly determines your registration bill.

1. AUTHORIZED CAPITAL VS. PAID-UP CAPITAL MECHANICS:
  • The ₹15 Lakh MCA Zero-Fee Window: On the MCA V3 portal, the Central Government charges ₹0 incorporation filing fee on SPICe+ (INC-32) for companies with an Authorized Share Capital up to ₹15,00,000 (you only pay ₹2,000 each on e-MoA and e-AoA if Authorized Capital exceeds ₹15 Lakhs, plus slab increases).
  • Avoiding the High Authorized Capital Trap: Setting a needlessly high Authorized Capital on Day 1 (e.g., ₹50 Lakhs when you only plan to deposit ₹1,00,000 in Paid-Up Capital) inflates your non-refundable State Stamp Duty at incorporation and raises your per-form ROC filing fee slab (₹200 vs. ₹500/₹600 per form) on every future compliance filing. Founders typically register with ₹1 Lakh to ₹10 Lakhs Authorized Capital and ₹10,000 to ₹1 Lakh Paid-Up Capital, increasing Authorized Capital later via Form SH-7 only when raising institutional capital.

03 / Registered Office Verification & Geo-Tagging Rules

Every registered entity in India must maintain a verifiable physical address capable of receiving official statutory notices under Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013 (or Section 13 of the LLP Act, 2008):

1. TEMPORARY CORRESPONDENCE ADDRESS VS. PERMANENT REGISTERED OFFICE (FORM INC-22): In SPICe+ Part B, founders who have not finalized a lease on Day 1 can select "No" to whether the correspondence address is the permanent Registered Office. Within 30 days of incorporation, the company must finalize its office and file Form INC-22 on MCA V3.
2. THE 3-DOCUMENT PROOF CHAIN:
  • Utility Bill (≤ 60 Days Old): Electricity bill, piped gas bill, water bill, or landline telephone bill (mobile internet bills are rejected). The exact spelling of the property owner's name and the complete premise address on the utility bill must match the NOC and Rent Agreement character-for-character.
  • No-Objection Certificate (NOC): Signed by the legal owner named on the utility bill explicitly permitting the company/LLP to use the address as its Registered Office.
  • Lease / Rent Agreement (or Ownership Sale Deed): Required if the premises are leased rather than owned by a director or their family member.
3. RESIDENTIAL FLATS VS. VIRTUAL OFFICES VS. INDUSTRIAL ESTATES:
  • Residential Flats: Legally permitted for incorporation on MCA V3 (using a parent's or spouse's electricity bill + NOC). However, if you later apply for GST registration involving physical inventory or State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) / Factories Act licenses, municipal zoning rules require shifting the address (or adding an "Additional Place of Business" in GST) to a commercial or notified industrial zone (GIDC/MIDC/KIADB).
  • Rule 25B Physical Verification & Signboard Mandate: Under Section 12(3) and Rule 25B, every company must paint or affix its Registered Name, CIN, and Complete Address outside its office in both English and the local vernacular language, and keep geo-tagged exterior and interior photographs with a director present in frame to survive ROC or GST physical verification drives.

04 / Identity, DSC Binding & Cross-Border Document Legalization

Since the transition to the MCA V3 system, all filings enforce automated database cross-validation against Income Tax PAN, DigiLocker, and Certifying Authority servers:

1. CLASS-3 DSC & MCA V3 "BUSINESS USER" BINDING: Class-2 DSCs are discontinued. Every director, designated partner, OPC nominee, and subscriber must obtain an individual Class-3 Digital Signature Certificate (Signing + Encryption) with video/biometric e-KYC (~₹1,000–₹1,500, valid for 1–2 years). Before signing SPICe+ or FiLLiP, each person must create a "Business User" account (not just a "Registered User" account) on mca.gov.in and associate their DSC USB token using the MCA E-Signer utility.
2. THE PAN VS. ID PROOF EXACT-MATCH RULE: In SPICe+ Part B and FiLLiP, the applicant's first, middle, and last name and Date of Birth are verified live against the CBDT Income Tax PAN database. If a director's PAN has initials (e.g., R. K. Sharma) while their Passport or Voter ID has expanded names (Ramesh Kumar Sharma), the form will trigger a validation or CRC resubmission error until the ID proofs are standardized.
3. RESIDENT DIRECTOR STATUTORY THRESHOLDS: Every Company (Section 149(3)) and LLP (Section 7) must have at least 1 Resident Director / Designated Partner who stayed in India for at least 182 days during the financial year (for OPCs, Rule 3 allows Non-Resident Indian citizens to incorporate an OPC with a relaxed 120-day residency threshold).
4. FOREIGN NATIONAL & FOREIGN PARENT LEGALIZATION MATRIX (RULE 13):
  • Hague Apostille Convention Countries (e.g., USA, UK, Singapore, EU, Australia): Passports, utility bills (≤ 2 months old), Foreign Parent Certificates of Incorporation, and Board Resolutions must be notarized and Apostilled in the home country.
  • Non-Hague Countries: Documents must be notarized, attested by the home Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and consularized by the Indian Embassy/High Commission.
  • Signing Inside India: If a foreign national signs incorporation documents while physically present in India, Apostille is waived only if they attach a copy of their Passport showing a valid Indian Business Visa (not a Tourist Visa) and the immigration arrival stamp.

06 / Allied Operational & Sectoral Registration Layer (AGILE-PRO-S & Beyond)

Every incorporated entity must navigate two tiers of allied registrations depending on its workforce, revenue, and capital structure:

1. BUNDLED REGISTRATIONS INSIDE SPICe+ (FORM AGILE-PRO-S / INC-35):

When filing SPICe+ for a Company, Form AGILE-PRO-S automatically generates:

  • PAN & TAN: Issued directly on the Certificate of Incorporation by the Income Tax Department.
  • EPFO & ESIC: Employer registration numbers are issued automatically at incorporation. Monthly return filings and contributions become mandatory once crossing 20 employees (for EPFO) or 10 employees (for ESIC).
  • Professional Tax (PTEC & PTRC): Automatically registered in applicable states (e.g., Maharashtra, Karnataka, West Bengal) to pay annual company enrollment tax (PTEC) and deduct employee professional tax (PTRC).
  • Shops & Establishments Registration & Bank Account Selection: Initiates state labor registration and routes KYC to the selected partner bank.
  • Optional GSTIN: Can be triggered inside AGILE-PRO-S if the Permanent Registered Office is finalized at incorporation, or applied separately via Form GST REG-01 on gst.gov.in.
2. UNBUNDLED MANDATORY & TRIGGER-BASED REGISTRATIONS:
  • Mandatory ISIN & Share Dematerialization (Rule 9A & Rule 9B): All Unlisted Public Companies (Rule 9A) and all Private Limited Companies that do not qualify as "Small Companies" (Rule 9B—such as Holding Companies, Subsidiary Companies, or WOS entities) must onboard with an RTA and a Depository (NSDL or CDSL), obtain an ISIN, and dematerialize 100% of promoter/director shares before issuing new shares, buying back shares, or transferring equity.
  • MSME Udyam Registration (udyamregistration.gov.in): Free post-incorporation registration for Proprietorships, Partnerships, LLPs, Co-operatives, and Companies (cannot be held by Section 8 NGOs or Trusts). Unlocks 50%–80% patent/trademark fee rebates and Section 43B(h) 45-day payment protection.
  • DPIIT Startup Recognition (nsws.gov.in): Available exclusively to Private Limited Companies, LLPs, and Registered Partnership Firms.
  • DGFT Import-Export Code (dgft.gov.in): Mandatory ₹500 lifetime PAN-based registration required before importing lab equipment, components, or receiving foreign export remittances.
  • Income Tax NGO / CSR Registrations: Form 10A / 10AB (for provisional and 5-year regular Section 12AB & 80G tax exemptions), NGO Darpan (ngodarpan.gov.in), MCA Form CSR-1 (to receive corporate CSR grants), and MHA FCRA Registration (fcraonline.nic.in, requiring 3 years of prior activity and ₹15 Lakh charity spend—or Prior Permission—to accept foreign philanthropic grants).

Section 8 Company (NGO) → Commercial Pvt Ltd Sec 8(4)(ii), Companies Act & Rules 21–22 (Form MGT-14 + Form INC-18 to Regional Director + Form INC-20) Extremely Restrictive: Requires Special Resolution, newspaper publication (Form INC-19), NOCs from Charity Commissioner/Income Tax/Sectoral Authorities, and paying an Exit Tax on Accreted Income (Sec 115TD) at the Maximum Marginal Rate (~34.94%–42.74%) plus surrendering assets bought via grants/exemptions. Prohibitive for commercializing deep-tech IP—confirming why commercial R&D should not be incorporated inside a Section 8 NGO.

08 / Resubmission Forensics & Clean Deregistration (C-PACE Strike-Off)

1. THE MCA V3 "TWO-RESUBMISSION" RULE (RULE 10):

When the Central Registration Centre (CRC) flags a defect in SPICe+ (INC-32) or FiLLiP, the applicant is granted 15 days to rectify and resubmit the form at ₹0 extra cost. If the resubmitted form still contains errors, the CRC grants one final 15-day resubmission window (maximum 2 resubmissions total). If the second resubmission fails, the entire application is permanently rejected and all stamp duties and fees paid are forfeited.

Top 5 CRC Resubmission Triggers to Audit Before Filing:

  1. Utility bill for Registered Office is older than 60 days or is a mobile/broadband bill instead of electricity/water/gas/landline.
  2. Property owner's name on the NOC has a minor spelling or initial mismatch compared to the electricity bill.
  3. Main Objects in e-MoA (INC-33) include financial/NBFC or multi-sector catch-all activities without an RBI/SEBI NOC, or fail to match the selected 5-digit NIC code.
  4. Subscriber or Director ID proof (Driving License/Voter ID/Passport) address does not match the residential address typed in SPICe+ Part B or their individual bank statement (≤ 2 months old).
  5. Foreign subscriber documents are notarized in their home country without an accompanying Hague Apostille sticker or Indian Embassy attestation.
2. CLEAN DEREGISTRATION & WINDING UP (AVOIDING DIRECTOR DISQUALIFICATION):

Abandoning an entity without filing annual returns for 3 consecutive financial years triggers Section 164(2) of the Companies Act, 2013—disqualifying founders from acting as a director in any Indian company for 5 years.

  • Closing a Sole Proprietorship (~24–48 Hours | ₹0 Fee): Surrender GST registration via Form GST REG-16 (if registered), cancel the Udyam certificate on udyamregistration.gov.in, and close the Current Bank Account.
  • Closing a Company via C-PACE Fast Track Exit (Section 248(2) | Form STK-2): Centralized through C-PACE (2 to 6 months). Prerequisite: Nil assets and liabilities, bank closure certificate, GST surrendered (Form GSTR-10), and all overdue filings (AOC-4 & MGT-7) completed. Submit Form STK-2 (₹10,000 fee) attaching Form STK-3 (notarized Indemnity Bond), Form STK-4 (sworn Affidavit), and Form STK-8 (CA-certified Statement of Accounts ≤ 30 days old). C-PACE issues public notice in Form STK-6 and final dissolution in Form STK-7 in the Official Gazette.
  • Closing an LLP via C-PACE (Section 75 & Rule 37 | LLP Form 24): Requires commercial inactivity for at least 1 full year, unanimous partner consent, bank closure, and filing overdue Form 8, Form 11, and Form 3. Submit LLP Form 24 (₹500 fee for Small LLPs; ₹1,000 for others) attaching a CA Nil Assets/Liabilities statement, partner indemnity bonds, and latest ITR acknowledgment.

Eight Newly Researched Advanced Registration & Statutory Mechanics

To ensure zero operational blind spots remain across the Indian registration ecosystem, this third block covers eight specialized registration mechanics that frequently stall deep-tech founders, academic spin-outs, and cross-border allocators after filing their initial incorporation forms.

09 / GST Biometric Authentication & GSK Physical Verification (Rule 8(4A) Mandate)

Applying for a GST number (either through AGILE-PRO-S during incorporation or separately via Form GST REG-01 on gst.gov.in) no longer relies solely on remote OTP verification. Under amended Rule 8(4A) of the CGST Rules, the Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN) enforces biometric and in-person document verification across Indian states:

1. PART A & PART B SUBMISSION (FORM GST REG-01): After verifying PAN, mobile, and email in Part A, the applicant completes Part B (uploading the Certificate of Incorporation, Registered Office electricity bill, owner NOC, and rent agreement).
2. OTP VS. BIOMETRIC GSK ROUTING (THE 15-DAY HARD WINDOW): If the GST portal's risk engine flags the application—or if the applicant opts out of online biometric identity OTP linkage—the Application Reference Number (ARN) is held back, and an automated intimation email is sent directing the Primary Authorized Signatory (PAS) and at least one Promoter/Director/Partner to book an appointment at a designated GST Suvidha Kendra (GSK). Within 15 calendar days of submitting Part B, both the PAS and the Director/Partner must physically visit the GSK carrying original identity cards, original PAN cards, original lease/NOC/utility bill, and appointment confirmation to complete live photo capture, fingerprint/iris biometric authentication, and original document verification. (If a Director has already completed biometric verification at a GSK for a previous GST registration in any state, they are exempt from repeating biometric capture, though the PAS must still verify original premise documents).
3. RULE 25 GEO-TAGGED PHYSICAL SITE INSPECTION: If the GST officer issues a query in Form GST REG-03, the startup has 7 working days to reply in Form GST REG-04 before receiving its Form GST REG-06 registration certificate.

10 / Academic / University Incubator Spin-Out & Faculty Director Registration Mechanics

When incorporating a deep-tech spin-out from an IIT, NIT, IISc, CSIR lab, or DST/MeitY Technology Business Incubator (TBI), standard SPICe+ filings fail unless three institutional registration rules are satisfied:

1. HOST INSTITUTE / TBI AS A DAY-1 CORPORATE SUBSCRIBER (SPICe+ PART B): Most academic incubators take 2% to 9.5% free-carry or seed equity in exchange for IP licensing or wet-lab incubation. If the TBI (usually a Section 8 Company or Registered Society) subscribes to shares at incorporation inside SPICe+ (INC-32), you must attach a certified copy of the TBI Governing Board Resolution authorizing the subscription and naming a designated official (e.g., the TBI CEO or Dean of R&D) to affix their Class-3 DSC on the e-MoA (INC-33) and e-AoA (INC-34). Pragmatic Workaround: To avoid waiting 4–8 weeks for a university board resolution before incorporating, founders typically incorporate the Pvt Ltd first with 2 individual founders, and then allot the TBI's shares post-incorporation via a Private Placement (Form PAS-3) or Rights Issue / Share Transfer (Form SH-4) upon signing the Incubation Agreement.
2. CENTRAL / STATE GOVERNMENT & AUTONOMOUS FACULTY NOCS: Professors and scientists at IITs, IISc, NITs, and CSIR labs are governed by the Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules (Rule 15) or equivalent university statutes, which prohibit holding an executive office of profit without sanction. Before a faculty member is listed as a Director in SPICe+ Part B, they must obtain a formal No-Objection Certificate (NOC) / Sabbatical Sanction Letter from the Institute Director/Board permitting them to act as a Non-Executive Director (or Executive Director if on approved entrepreneurial lien/sabbatical) and attach it alongside their Form DIR-2 consent.

11 / Press Note 3 (PN3) & Land-Border Beneficial Ownership Clearance in Registration

Under Rule 6(a) of the FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 (Press Note 3 of 2020) and amended Rule 8 / Rule 12 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, strict national security screening applies at the moment of registration:

THE TRIGGER COUNTRIES: China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan, and Afghanistan.
THREE REGISTRATION LOCKS:
  1. Direct or Indirect Subscription (SPICe+ & Form FC-GPR): If any proposed shareholder, foreign parent company, or Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO)—even holding a fractional indirect stake through a Delaware, Cayman, or Singapore fund—is a citizen of, or incorporated in, a land-bordering country, the 100% Automatic FDI Route is blocked. Prior government approval must be obtained via the National Single Window System (NSWS) / Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP) with Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) clearance before allotting shares.
  2. Director Appointment Lock (Form DIR-2 / Form DIR-3 / SPICe+): Under Rule 10A of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules, 2014, no national of a land-bordering country can obtain a DIN or be appointed as a Director in an Indian company without attaching a prior Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) Security Clearance directly inside Form DIR-3 or SPICe+.
  3. Mandatory PN3 Self-Declaration: Every SPICe+ incorporation and every RBI FIRMS Form FC-GPR filing requires an explicit checkbox/declaration confirming whether prior Government approval under Press Note 3 is triggered.

12 / DIN Lifecycle, Annual DIR-3 KYC-Web & Disqualification Forensics

A Director Identification Number (DIN) or Designated Partner Identification Number (DPIN) is a lifetime 8-digit statutory identity allotted by the MCA, governed by strict maintenance rules:

1. ALLOTMENT PATHWAYS (SPICe+ VS. FORM DIR-3): Up to 3 new DINs (in SPICe+ INC-32) or 2 new DPINs (in Form FiLLiP) can be generated automatically at incorporation. If you need to add a 4th first-time director at incorporation—or appoint a new director after incorporation—that individual must apply for a DIN via Form DIR-3 on MCA V3 (₹500 fee), which must be digitally counter-signed by an existing Director/Company Secretary of the company proposing to appoint them, followed by filing Form DIR-12 within 30 days of appointment.
2. THE ANNUAL SEPTEMBER 30 DIR-3 KYC LOCK: Every individual holding a DIN/DPIN allotted on or before March 31 of a financial year must complete their annual KYC on or before September 30 of the immediate next financial year—even if they are not currently an active director in any company. First-time KYC (or mobile/email update) requires e-Form DIR-3 KYC (Class-3 DSC + CA/CS/CMA certification). Subsequent years without changes are completed in 2 minutes via DIR-3 KYC-Web on MCA V3 using mobile and email OTPs (₹0 fee). Deactivation Penalty: Missing the September 30 deadline automatically marks the DIN as "Deactivated due to non-filing of DIR-3 KYC," blocking the founder from signing any MCA form across all their companies until reactivated by paying a flat ₹5,000 statutory penalty.

13 / Post-Incorporation Bank KYC, ICEGATE AD Code & Customs MOOWR Registration

For hardware, biotech, polymers, and electronics startups importing specialized components or exporting R&D deliverables, three post-incorporation customs and banking registrations are required:

1. COMPLETING PHYSICAL BANK IN-PERSON VERIFICATION (IPV): Selecting a bank inside Form AGILE-PRO-S during SPICe+ filing only initiates a provisional lead. Under RBI Master Direction on KYC, a bank officer must still conduct a physical In-Person Verification (IPV) at your Registered Office, verify your exterior Company Nameplate (Section 12), and collect a physical wet-signed Board Resolution, Certificate of Incorporation, e-MoA, e-AoA, and PAN before activating outgoing NEFT/RTGS/SWIFT permissions.
2. AUTHORIZED DEALER (AD) CODE REGISTRATION ON ICEGATE (icegate.gov.in): Obtaining a DGFT Import-Export Code (IEC) is not enough to clear a shipment through Indian Customs or receive an export e-BRC (Electronic Bank Realization Certificate). You must obtain a 14-digit Authorized Dealer (AD) Code Letter on bank letterhead from your corporate bank branch, register on the ICEGATE Portal (icegate.gov.in) with your Class-3 DSC, and link that AD Code to the specific Customs Ports/Airports (e.g., Nhava Sheva, Mumbai Air Cargo, Mundra, Bengaluru Air Cargo) where your shipments arrive or depart.
3. MOOWR (MANUFACTURE AND OTHER OPERATIONS IN WAREHOUSE) LICENSE (SECTION 65, CUSTOMS ACT): Registered online via the Invest India / CBIC Bonded Warehouse Portal (bond.cbic.gov.in) under the Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse Regulations, 2019. Converts your factory or R&D pilot floor into a licensed Customs Bonded Warehouse—allowing you to defer 100% of Basic Customs Duty (BCD) and IGST on imported capital machinery (indefinitely until cleared into the domestic market) and raw components (completely waived if exported, or paid only when the finished product is sold domestically).

14 / Depository (NSDL/CDSL) ISIN Activation & Consolidated Share Stamp Duty

Whenever a startup raises an angel or VC round, converts from a Pvt Ltd to a Public Ltd, or becomes a Holding/Subsidiary Company (losing its "Small Company" exemption under Rule 9B), physical paper share certificates are replaced by mandatory depository registration:

1. TRIPARTITE AGREEMENT (COMPANY + RTA + NSDL/CDSL): The company appoints a SEBI-registered Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) (e.g., Link Intime, KFinTech, Purva Sharegistry, or NextGen), executes a Tripartite Agreement with NSDL or CDSL, and pays the admission fee, security deposit, and annual custodial fee (~₹15,000–₹35,000 total) to generate a 12-character ISIN (International Securities Identification Number) for each class of equity or CCPS shares.
2. CENTRALIZED SHARE ALLOTMENT STAMP DUTY (0.005%): Under the amended Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (Section 9A), stamp duty on the issue of new shares or CCPS is standardized nationwide at 0.005% of the total market value of the securities allotted (and 0.015% on share transfers). For demat shares, this stamp duty is collected automatically by the Depository (NSDL/CDSL) before crediting shares into the investor's demat account; for physical shares in an exempt Small Company, it is paid via the state e-stamping portal or SHCIL (Stock Holding Corporation of India) before issuing Form SH-1 share certificates and filing Form PAS-3 (Return of Allotment, due within 15 days of allotment).
